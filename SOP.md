# Statement of Purpose

During my R&D internship at Synopsys, I built a manipulation system with its digital twin for automated pick-and-place tasks. While deploying reinforcement learning models for sim-to-real transfer, I noticed something counterintuitive: increasing model complexity degraded performance under minor environmental variations. This failure exposed my limited understanding of how learning-based controllers handle uncertainty in real-world settings. Since then, I have focused on understanding robustness in robotic control—not through more data or bigger models, but through principled reasoning about uncertainty. The IRIS program at NUS offers the research training I need to develop this understanding rigorously.

I am a pre-final year Mechanical Engineering student at IIEST Shibpur, ranked in the top 1% of my department. My coursework in robot kinematics and machine design provided the foundations, but my interest in robot learning crystallized through project work. Under Prof. Shyamal Kumar Chatterjee, I studied singular configurations of the 3-3R parallel manipulator using numerical methods in MATLAB—an early encounter with the constraints physical systems impose on control. Later, working on vision-language-action control of a Franka Panda arm, I found that semantic ambiguity in language instructions led to unpredictable behaviour. I introduced simple confidence measures that reliably distinguished successful from failed executions, which taught me that explicit uncertainty representation matters more than implicit end-to-end learning.

At Synopsys under Dr. Prith Banerjee, I worked on integrating IT and OT systems through digital twins. After an initial coffee vending automation attempt proved unreliable, we pivoted to a simpler manipulation setup. I built the robot model and scene in SolidWorks and Blender, then deployed it in Isaac Sim with custom gripper physics. While training RL policies showed promise, I realized my shallow grasp of learning-based control limited my ability to make informed design decisions. This experience clarified that I needed structured research training to bridge the gap between simulation and deployment—precisely what IRIS offers.

I have also worked on remote sensing problems. Collaborating with Anuvab Sen from Georgia Tech, I developed resource-efficient state space models for satellite data classification, resulting in a first-author paper at IEEE IGARSS 2025. I also explored sequence learning with Mamba models on fossil datasets, presented at IEEE IGARSS 2025. These experiences taught me how to structure problems across different domains, but they also reinforced that my primary interest lies in embodied intelligence—robots that act reliably in the physical world.

I want to pursue a research career focused on building robot learning systems that reason explicitly about uncertainty rather than relying on scale alone. Current approaches in sim-to-real transfer often fail because they treat the reality gap as noise to be averaged out rather than structure to be modeled. I am particularly interested in how control policies can maintain performance guarantees despite distribution shift—a question that sits at the intersection of learning theory, control theory, and system identification. Long-term, I aim to lead a research group developing principled methods for deploying learning-based robots in unstructured environments, with applications in manufacturing, logistics, and human-centered assistance.

The IRIS program is the ideal next step. NUS has strong research in robot learning, particularly in manipulation and sim-to-real transfer, which aligns directly with my interests. The program's emphasis on structured research training will give me the theoretical foundations and experimental discipline I currently lack. I am confident that my undergraduate work—spanning kinematics, vision-language integration, digital twins, and RL—has prepared me for the rigor of graduate-level research. Through IRIS, I aim to develop the depth needed to contribute meaningfully to robust robot learning.
