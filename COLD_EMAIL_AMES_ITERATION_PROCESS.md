During my R&D internship at Ansys, I worked on control, digital twin, and simulation-driven robotic systems, where I encountered the fundamental tension between achieving high performance and maintaining safety in complex dynamical systems. Your CLF-CBF-QP framework addresses this beautifully by combining Control Lyapunov Functions for stabilization with Control Barrier Functions for safety, synthesized in real-time through quadratic programming. The key insight that barrier functions can act as safety filters for learning-enabled controllers is particularly compelling—it allows robots to learn increasingly capable behaviors without compromising safety.

This becomes especially challenging in bipedal locomotion. Walking and running involve complex hybrid dynamics with discrete contact events and continuous evolution between contacts. Traditional model-based approaches require precise knowledge of these dynamics, which is difficult to obtain and doesn't generalize well to new terrains or disturbances. Your work on hybrid zero dynamics provides a principled way to handle these contact transitions, and your recent research on learning-based approaches for agile locomotion shows how data-driven methods can complement this framework.

In my own work, I've focused on state-space models and deep learning for capturing complex dynamics in robotic systems. Through simulation-based projects in robotic manipulation and motion planning, I've seen how learning can accelerate control design but also introduces challenges in verification and safety assurance. I'm particularly interested in how learned models of contact dynamics could be integrated with barrier function-based safety filters. A concrete question I've been thinking about: when robots learn locomotion gaits through reinforcement learning, how can we construct barrier functions that adapt to the learned policy while maintaining formal safety guarantees? Could we learn barrier functions jointly with the control policy, perhaps using demonstrations of safe locomotion?
