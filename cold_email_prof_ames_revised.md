Dear Prof. Ames,

I'm Maneet Chatterjee, a 3rd-year mechanical engineering undergraduate at IIESTS, applying through Caltech SURF to work in your AMBER Lab this summer. Your research on integrating learning-based methods with formal safety guarantees for legged robots directly addresses challenges I've encountered in robotic control and autonomous systems.

**Background from Ansys R&D:**

During my R&D internship at Ansys, I developed control algorithms and digital twin simulations for robotic systems. Three aspects directly relate to your work:

- **Trajectory optimization** for robotic manipulators, which exposed me to contact-rich dynamics and real-time constraint satisfaction
- **Safety verification** through workspace analysis, where I saw how traditional reachability methods fail to scale with learning-based controllers
- **Digital twin development** for sim-to-real validation, which taught me the importance of accurate dynamics models

These experiences revealed a critical gap: while learning excels at capturing complex dynamics, integrating it with formal safety guarantees remains challenging—exactly what your research addresses.

**Papers That Shaped My Interest:**

Your CLF-CBF-QP framework elegantly solves this. Reading "Control Barrier Functions: Theory and Applications," I was struck by how barrier functions provide real-time safety through a simple derivative inequality that composes naturally with optimization-based control. Your recent work on safety filters for learning-enabled controllers extends this to learned dynamics, and your papers on hybrid systems address how to maintain safety across discrete contact transitions in legged locomotion.

What particularly resonates is your work on "Learning Certified Control Using Contraction Metrics"—the idea that we can learn stability certificates alongside policies rather than post-hoc verification could enable more agile behaviors while preserving guarantees.

**Robotics Projects and Insights:**

In my multi-robot coordination work, I implemented MPC-based collision avoidance but faced two challenges: real-time computation as systems scaled, and safety guarantees under uncertain dynamics. Your CLF-CBF-QP approach addresses both through efficient QP solving and handling bounded uncertainty.

This made me wonder: could similar principles apply to multi-contact scenarios in locomotion? Each contact point introduces constraints analogous to multi-agent collision avoidance. The parallels between distributed coordination and multi-contact force distribution intrigue me.

**How I Can Contribute:**

I see concrete ways to contribute to your lab's research:

1. **Learning contact dynamics:** My publications on state-space models could support learning contact models for legged robots, particularly using recurrent architectures to capture temporal dependencies in contact sequences.

2. **Simulation infrastructure:** My digital twin experience could help develop sim-to-real pipelines for testing learned locomotion controllers, accelerating development and providing safe initial learning environments.

3. **Multi-contact insights:** My multi-agent coordination work might inform how we coordinate multiple contact points in whole-body control.

**Research Questions I'm Exploring:**

- Can we learn barrier functions jointly with policies to achieve tighter margins and more agile locomotion?
- How do learned contraction metrics compare to fixed Lyapunov functions for stability guarantees?
- How can we handle combinatorial contact sequences in learning-based planners while maintaining safety?

I'm applying through Caltech SURF and would be excited to contribute to the AMBER Lab's work on safe, agile legged robots. I'm particularly interested in ongoing projects on learning-based control for dynamic locomotion.
